## Q1. Which offer can be sold earliest, and to whom?

### Who pays: decorators, not homeowners

- **Homeowners have nothing to buy from us.** Their job is to get a decorator; the decorator's quote is free to them. Our keyword data shows homeowner intent around price ("how much does a painter and decorator charge per day", "how much should i pay for a painter and decorator": Google UK autocomplete, checked 18/10). That is price research, not intent to buy software. The only homeowner money would be paint-retailer affiliate links on the calculator. That pays a small commission, needs approval and traffic we don't have, and pulls the brand towards DIY (**not verified**: no affiliate terms checked). **0 hours on homeowner monetisation in 2026** [opinion].
- **Decorators** already pay for tools in this category. Queezy is £8.99, Paint Manager £19.99–£39.99/month (`answers-2026-10-17.md`). Paid UK pricing books exist: Spon's *Finishings: Painting, Decorating…* costs £68.99 ([professionalbooks.co.uk](https://professionalbooks.co.uk/pricing-guides/painting-decorating), checked 18/10). So they pay. The open question is whether they pay **us**, **now**.

**Evidence:**

## Q5. Risks

| Risk | What could happen | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand dilution** | An Etsy shop of Word templates beside a free online tool reads as "template seller", not "the decorator's quote app" [opinion] | Don't open it in 2026. If ever, use a separate shop name or wait until the app sells |
| **Homeowner traffic that never pays** | The calculator draws DIY and homeowner searches; the watermark is seen by clients, not decorators | Measure `calc_room_added ÷ calc_used` (funnel 2b). Put 0 hours into homeowner offers. The CTA always speaks to decorators |
| **Consumer law: who is a consumer** | A consumer is an individual acting "wholly or mainly outside" their trade (CCR 2013 reg 4, **not re-opened today**). A decorator buying a business tool is probably **not** a consumer, so the 14-day right mostly doesn't apply. A homeowner who buys would be one [opinion, not legal advice] | Don't target homeowners. Polar handles checkout terms |
| **Digital content: right to cancel** | For digital content, the trader may not begin supply in the 14 days unless the consumer gave **express consent** and **acknowledged losing the right to cancel**. Without that, the consumer pays nothing if they cancel ([CCR reg 37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37), checked 18/10). Software access may count as a **service** instead (reg 36: proportionate payment), which is unclear for a one-off "for as long as it runs" plan. **Not verified** | Ask Polar whether its checkout captures the reg 37 consent and acknowledgement. Never write "non-refundable". The accepted line ("doesn't renew… 60 days' notice… CSV export") is fine. Statutory rights can't be removed (CRA 2015) [opinion] |
| **Info requirements** | Distance sales need trader identity, geographic address, price incl. taxes and cancellation info (CCR reg 13/Sch 2, **not re-opened**). The E-Commerce Regs reg 6 address question is open (CEO P2) | Settle it with the terms and privacy policy before checkout opens (already the lead's task, before 21/11) |
| **VAT on digital sales** | Polar is MoR, prices incl. VAT, set to Inclusive (PRD). Gumroad is MoR from 01/01/2025 ([pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing)). Etsy UK VAT on digital items: third-party sources only ([commenda](https://www.commenda.io/uk/etsy-vat), [linkmybooks](https://linkmybooks.com/blog/etsy-vat)), **not verified** on Etsy's own pages (403) | Sell only through an MoR. Keep "incl. VAT" in every price |
| **Polar rejects or delays the account** | No money at all, whatever the marketing | Apply this week; Plan B as above |
| **Fake urgency** | Showing "X of 50" or pre-selling before checkout works | CEO P1. Line D mentions no places and no deadline |

Waiting for 22/11 bets everything on one launch week and on Polar approval. If Polar is slow, December may arrive with nothing tested. That risk is bigger now that the Facebook group is gone (18/10).

And "tested willingness to pay £9 for a Word file" tells us little about paying for the app. Still, the Polar risk is real, which is why the Polar application is item 1 and Plan B is written down.
