## 1. T18: November vs labour-only now

**Decision:** add the official cancellation text to the quote PDF before 31/10 (labour-only), or later for both job types. **At stake for 31/12:** nothing on the payment path. The quote template is a free SEO tool. The risk is wrong legal text on a decorator's document (CEO risk #2, 18/10), plus hours in a plan that "only just" fits.

**I checked the lead's legal claim myself (18/10/2026):**
- [fact] [reg 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/5): a sales contract includes any contract that has both goods and services as its object. A service contract is any contract that isn't a sales contract. A distance contract needs exclusive use of distance communication up to the moment of agreement. An on-premises contract is anything that is neither distance nor off-premises.
- [fact] [reg 30](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/30): for service contracts the 14 days run from the contract. For sales contracts they run from when the goods arrive (or the last item, if delivered on different days).
- [fact] [reg 28(3)(c)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28): in a sales contract, the right ends once the goods are inseparably mixed with other items after delivery. Applied paint is a likely case [opinion]. How this works for a mixed supply-and-apply job is **not verified**.
- [fact] [reg 36](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36): it talks about "the supply of a service". Para (6) says the consumer pays nothing if the cancellation information wasn't given. That backs the T4 FAQ sentence.
- [fact] [BIS guidance, Dec 2013](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/429300/bis-13-1368-consumer-contracts-information-cancellation-and-additional-payments-regulations-guidance.pdf), read in full text:
  - Section A para 3: mixed contracts such as supply and fitting are sales contracts. The goods rules apply to the goods and the services rules apply to the services.
  - Para 4: the test is whether transfer of ownership of the goods is a key objective.
  - Section H para 2: the 14 days for goods start after the goods arrive.
  - Section H para 10 (dishwasher example): the consumer cancels within 14 days of delivery but pays for the installation if they asked for an early start.
- [fact] [Business Companion, off-premises guide](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/consumer-contracts/consumer-contracts-off-premises-sales) (reviewed Feb 2025):
  - A sales contract covers goods and services supplied together.
  - **New point:** a trader who visits, leaves a quote (or sends one later), and is accepted later has made an **on-premises** contract, with no cancellation right. It's off-premises only if the consumer agrees immediately after the visit.
- [fact] [Herefordshire Trading Standards](https://www.herefordshire.gov.uk/business/trading-standards-and-consumer-advice-contact-details/selling-away-from-premises/) (no date shown): home improvement work that supplies goods gives 14 days from the last goods received.
- [fact] [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/getting-home-improvements-done/cancelling-building-or-decorating-work/) (decorating page) gives the same goods-delivery rule.
- [fact] Reg 27(3A) (pending) comes from the [DMCC Act 2024 s.279(6)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/279). It excludes **subscription** contracts and is marked prospective. It doesn't touch one-off decorating jobs. This closes the "not verified" note in my R4.
- [fact] The [Schedule 3 page](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/schedule/3) loads and is image-only. It lists one amendment, a currency change in paragraph 5(b) in force 31/12/2020. That matches `legal/README.md`, though I didn't check the amending SI's number.

**Verdict on the lead's claim: right.** I found no official source saying supply-and-apply decorating is a service contract. BIS para 4 leaves room to argue that paint isn't a "key objective" [opinion], but no one official makes that argument for decorating. **My R4 said "services wording" for every quote. That was wrong for the template's default job type.**

It scopes the right to contracts agreed in the client's home, which matches Business Companion. The early-start sentence is backed by reg 36(6) and BIS H para 10 (services in a mixed contract).
